# Conclude your n-tier workload migration from on-premises to Azure

This article is part of a series about how to [migrate an n-tier workload from on-premises to Azure](/azure/migration/migrate-n-tier-from-on-premises-introduction). 

Workload migration is an intensive project, especially if it's your first migration or the workload has many dependencies. This series helped you find the right migration approach and prepared you to handle problems.

The series covered the following items that you need for success:

- A concrete and well-reasoned plan
- Involvement, alignment, and final approval from the relevant stakeholders
- A solid target architecture
- The right migration strategy for each workload component
- A phased approach and clear checklists

> [!TIP] 
> Remember that you can rely on experts. When possible, work with colleagues, Microsoft experts, or Microsoft partners who successfully migrated on-premises workloads to Azure. For example, reach out to cloud solutions architects or the Microsoft Industry Solutions Delivery team. Their experience can help you navigate challenges and adopt best practices.
>
> Ask Microsoft or your partner whether [Cloud Accelerate Factory](https://partner.microsoft.com/asset/collection/cloud-accelerate-factory#/) can help for future migration work. Confirm current eligibility, available assistance, and delivery responsibilities before you rely on it.

Migration requires effort, coordination, and cooperation across teams. After you successfully migrate and decommission your on-premises workload, celebrate your win.
